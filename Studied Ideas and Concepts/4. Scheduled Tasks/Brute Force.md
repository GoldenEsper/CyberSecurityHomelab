## What is it?
A method of attempting to access an account my trying multiple different passwords until successful authentication is gained. Often used to imply the usage of computational power and trial and error.
### Examples in Modern Contexts:
1. **Guessing Passwords:** The purpose of a brute force attack might be to guess a user's password in order to access a secure service.
2. **Cracking Encryption:** Attackers might try multiple encryption keys until they find the correct one to reveal confidential data.
3. **Social Media:** Less tech-savvy attackers might use brute force on lock screens or other physical security measures to access mobile devices.
### Mitigation Techniques:
1. **Complexity:** Using characters other than letters and numbers (symbols), capital letters, and a mix of cases can increase the difficulty to guess.
2. **Length:** Longer passwords are harder to crack.
3. **Use of Password Managers:** Random password generation ensures that each password is unique and complex.
4. **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** Adding another layer of identity verification makes brute force attacks less practical.
5. **Rate Limiting:** Limiting the number of login attempts, especially on failed logins, can prevent brute force attacks.
